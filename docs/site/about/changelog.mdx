Glassine moves in days rather than versions so far. The newest day below is the direct download; the Mac App Store has the same code without the updater, as of its own day, and each new version follows it there once Apple has looked. Both hold everything else below, including the lines marked *after build 7*, which went in after the earlier store build of that number. Everything here is in the source on `main`.

<Update label="November 2, 2026">
- **Every question is asked on the glass.** Rename, New Folder, *Move to the Trash?* and anything that went wrong used to drop in as the Mac's own grey sheet from the title bar — the one surface in the app not cut from its glass. They are cards on the page now, in the look's colours, with the name in a field of the app's own and the answers as capsules: Return answers, Esc keeps things as they were, and so does a click on the page behind. The page's editor gives up the keyboard while a card is up, so Return goes to the card and not to a new line.
- **A page still on its way from iCloud says so.** A document iCloud had not brought down yet opened as an empty page with *Start writing* on it, and anything typed there was quietly dropped — nothing could be kept under text that was about to arrive. The page now says *Coming down from iCloud…* at the foot, in place of the save dot, and is read-only until the file lands. A file that could not be read at all opened blank as well, and the first keystroke would have saved that blank page over it; it now says *Couldn't read the file*, stays read-only, and is never written.
- **The fanned pages keep to the page.** ⌥⇥ laid the open pages out across the whole window, so with the sidebar showing the leftmost card sat half over it. The fan is centred over the page now, clipped to it, and the sidebar steps back with the page while the cards are up. With one page open, the name in the footer gives the small shake the pips used to be credited with — one page draws no pips to shake.
- **The glass is a handle again.** Dragging the window by its glass was turned off in 0.2.28 for what it cost on every frame of every animation. The useful part of it is back at no cost until a press lands: the margin beside the editor's column, and the empty ground of the wall, the Timelapse and the sidebar, carry the window when pressed and moved; a press that stays is a click, and in the margin it puts the caret where a click there always did.
- **A page beside the editor no longer renders on every keystroke.** With the page beside the editor, each keystroke rendered the whole document to HTML on the main thread — and then read the result five times over to tell what had changed — before the page was told to wait for typing to pause. The render now waits for the pause and happens off the main thread; only the newest one lands. A box ticked on the page beside the editor was also lost at the next keystroke, since the editor was never told the line had changed; it is told now, and so is a task that sinks.
- **Lighter keystrokes.** The caret's own bookkeeping read the whole document several times per keystroke: the text storage's string, asked for from Swift, is copied on every read, and the editor asked a dozen times along the way — once per line break during layout, too. It reads the text in place now. The file's name is taken from the first line after every keystroke; finding that line used to split the whole document into lines and compile five regular expressions each time, and now stops at the first line that counts. The caret's position was written into the settings every 0.8 s of writing, for a restore that never came, and each write had every view in the window redo its body; it is not written. A date capsule's draw walked the document's attributes to its end when there was no capsule in view; it looks only at the stretch on screen. With the syntax hidden, every chunk of glyphs found the caret's sentence again; it reads the one the editor keeps. The fence scan that follows a keystroke in a document with a code block in it made and trimmed a substring for every paragraph; it reads them in place. The month in the sidebar rebuilt its grid on every keystroke of a daily note; it rebuilds when the word count changes. And a harness note along the keystroke's path built its line even outside a run.
- **Dragging the sidebar's edge, or the divider beside the page, no longer rewrites the settings on every frame.** The width lives with the drag until it ends and is written once — before, every frame of a drag was a settings change, and a settings change has every view in the window redo its body.
- **One glass for every card.** Settings, the command bar, the shortcut sheet and the prompts are cut from one recipe now (`Glass.swift`) — the same blur, veil, hairline and shadow — where each had carried a copy a few hundredths apart. The command bar's highlight glides between rows, as the manual said it did, and the row in force — the style on, the look worn, the sort in use — carries its mark at the end of the row rather than in its name. The motion the rest of the window makes — a card coming up, a page sliding aside, the corridor walking, a star springing — now stands still under Reduce Motion with the rest.
- **Small things.** The counter at the foot of the page brightens under the pointer, so that a click changes what it counts is no longer a secret. *Copied…* comes in beside the page's name rather than in its place. The wall's card menu has *Copy Path*, as the sidebar's does. File → **Star**, and *Star*, *Rename…* and *Duplicate* in the command bar. A card no longer repeats its heading under its name when the name is the heading with a `?`, `/` or `:` taken out. *Float on Top* is called that everywhere. The README and the manual say fourteen Review styles and eight looks, as there are; ⌥⌘D, retired in September, is gone from the README's table; the Welcome page says fourteen too.
- **Under the hood.** A write forced to the disk (a move, a duplicate, a trip to the Trash) now waits for a write already on its way, so the newer text is the one the file is left with. A page's save state is set only when it changes. The theme lookup every view makes a few times per pass is kept between changes. The wall's key handler held the navigator in a cycle; a document's four-second save timer was added to the run loop twice; switching libraries left the old library's publisher attached. Dead code gone: a flag written three times and never read, a font nobody drew with, two library properties nothing looked at, a parent-folder helper the Shelf already had, an unused view modifier, an empty-library view nothing showed, a regular expression compiled on every Return.
</Update>

<Update label="October 19, 2026">
- **Tables are tables.** A pipe table used to show as hairlines where its pipes were and one rule under the header — open at the top and the bottom, nothing between the rows, and a long cell wrecked it: a column was padded to its widest cell however long, so a key-and-paragraph table pushed every row's closing pipe off the page onto a line of its own, rows two lines tall, stubs under the words. It is drawn as a grid now, the way Craft or Notion would: a border with rounded corners, a line under every row and at every column, the header in bold on a faint fill, room kerned round every cell's words. A table is never wider than the page — the widest columns are narrowed first, together — and a cell longer than its column wraps under it, by the row's head indent, with its lines ending at the border by the row's tail indent, so the pipe after a cell that only just overflows no longer drops onto a line of its own; its closing pipe is laid out at no width for the same reason. A long cell in an earlier column wraps too, and the cells after it in that row follow on its last line, the grid leaving those columns open for the row. The separator is a thin strip the header's line is drawn through, and Tab skips it. The table is laid out again when the column changes width.
- **Smart dashes leave a separator alone.** Typing `| --- | --- |` with smart dashes on turned each `---` into an em dash as the space after it arrived, and the table came apart; `---` on a line of its own was already protected. On a line of pipes, dashes, colons and spaces the substitution now stands down for every keystroke, an em dash it has already made is turned back into hyphens, and a separator written with em or en dashes still reads as one, in the editor and in Review.
- **A paste straightens a separator, makes a table of tabs, lands on its own lines.** A separator that arrived as `| — | — |` from somewhere else gets `---` back. Plain text whose every line has the same number of tabs — a spreadsheet selection, a table a page handed over as text — becomes a pipe table. A paste of more than one line goes on lines of its own rather than joining the first row to the words before the caret. Text dragged in from another app is read like a paste; words dragged across the page carry Glassine's own mark and are not read for their style a second time.
- **`/table 3x4`.** The slash menu's Table takes a size — columns, or columns × rows with the header counted — and sits in the first screen of the menu now, which shows nine items rather than eight.
- **A heading keeps its room above.** With the syntax hidden, a heading pasted in — or written straight under a paragraph — sat flush against the line above until the caret visited it, and then kept its room. A hidden marker was a null glyph, and the typesetter lays null glyphs at the start of a paragraph into the line before them, after its newline, and then counts that line as neither the end of its paragraph nor the start of the next: no spacing on either side. The caret's visit laid the line out again from its own boundary, which is why it came right. A marker at the start of a paragraph — a heading's `## `, a quote's `>`, a task's `- ` — is now a control glyph of no advance, which starts the paragraph's own line, so the room is there from the first layout.
- **No half-there card in the Timelapse.** With *Everything* showing, a day's note begun the morning after — Tuesday's note, started Wednesday — stood under Wednesday as well as Tuesday, and both cards claimed to be the source of the open-zoom, which left the second a sliver of a plate. A note stands under its own day and nowhere else now, and a day left with nothing on it is no day of the corridor.
- **Tables on the cards.** A document's card — on the wall, among the fanned pages, in a pip's preview — showed a table as its raw pipes. It draws it as a small grid now: up to four columns and five rows, the header on its fill, each cell one line, a note counting what is left out. The wall's cards keep their one run of text; only a card with a table in it splits the run around the grid.
- **Closing pages.** Every document opened stays open as a page, so the stack grew until the fan was a bazillion cards. ⌥⌘W (File → Close Other Pages) keeps the page on screen and closes the rest; ⌥⇧⌘W (File → Close All Pages) empties the stack and leaves All Documents. With the pages fanned out the card under the highlight has a × in its corner that closes it and keeps the fan up, beside ⌫ which did that already; a pip in the footer has the three in its right-click menu, and ⌥-click on a pip closes that page. The command bar lists the two new ones.
</Update>

<Update label="September 5, 2026">
- Date chips draw as one capsule again. A token whose `@` is dimmer than its date could reach the layout manager as two runs, each drawing its own capsule, overlapping.
- The Today view is now **Timelapse**, in the sidebar, the View menu, the command bar, the shortcut sheet and here.
- **⌘1, ⌘2 and ⌘3** open the three rows at the top of the sidebar in order: All Documents, Timelapse, New Document. ⌘P, ⌘D and ⌘N still work. The rows are reordered to match and show the numbers.
- Gallery cards animate their own entrance, so a mosaic removed mid-cascade can no longer leave a ghost behind.
- Timelapse: every day is the same size whatever it holds, each fades out at its lower edge before it reaches the day in front, so the cards never touch or overlap, and the days sit close — a fifth of a card apart, whatever the window's height. Today starts at the middle of the window; the days before it climb the upper half. Pressing ⌘1 then ⌘2 no longer leaves both sidebar rows lit.
- The self-photographing mode (used to check layout without anyone at the Mac) now asks the window server for the app's own window only, so macOS no longer treats each picture as screen recording and says so; the store pictures keep the fuller composite by request.
- **Float on top.** ⌘. (View → Float on Top) keeps the window over other apps' windows and on screen across Stage Manager sets; a pin in the footer shows while it floats. Remembered between launches.
- This documentation site, at [docs.glassine.ink](https://docs.glassine.ink), and the front door at [glassine.ink](https://glassine.ink).
- App Store build 0.2.3 (7), with all of the above, submitted for review in answer to App Review's request for information about the first build.
- *After build 7.* **Formatting as you go.** A bar appears over selected text — bold, italic, strike, code, chip, link, H1–H3, bullet, numbered, task, quote — and acts on every paragraph a selection touches. A `/` at the start of a line opens a menu of headings, lists, task, quote, code block, divider, link, chip and dates, filtered by what you type after it. **Chips**: ⌘⇧H puts the selected words in a capsule like a date's, written as `==word==`, drawn in Review and the PDF too. **Hide Markdown syntax** (⌃⌘M, View menu, Settings → Editor) takes the marks off the page except in the sentence being written; bullets draw as bullets. See [formatting](/editor/formatting). The caret's glide time now goes up to 500 ms.
- *After build 7.* Sorting by **date created** works: every save used to put a new file in the old one's place, which made a document's creation date its last save, so the order was the same as *Last edited*. Saves now carry the creation date across. Documents already in the library keep the dates they have, which is when they were last saved before this build; new ones and everything from here on are right.
- *After build 7.* **glassine.ink, remade.** One window travels down the page as you scroll — the film, then each picture in turn, beside the words about it — and comes back up the same way; the page is lit by the app's own backdrop, drawn by the same shader, and the last part lets you change it. Headings in the Book page's face.
- *After build 7.* **⌘→ stops at the end of the line.** With the syntax hidden, a line followed by a task, a heading or a quotation sent the caret to the start of that next line instead, and ⌘⇧→ selected into it. A hidden marker is a glyph that takes no room, and the typesetter puts those at the start of a paragraph into the line above, after its newline — so the end of that line's glyphs, where AppKit's ⌘→ goes, lay past the newline and the marker. The end of the line is now worked out by the editor and never lies beyond the caret's own paragraph; the last line of a wrapped paragraph had the same fault.
- *After build 7.* **The icon is the glass.** The app icon's sheet is now a square of Glassine's own window — the Dusk theme over the Aurora backdrop, folds of indigo, magenta and teal, taken by the app photographing itself (`tests/shot-icon.sh` → `Resources/aurora.png`; `make_icon.py --aurora` draws the wordmark's g on it in paper-white, with a soft shadow so the letter sits on the bright folds). One icon for both appearances now; the paper and charcoal sheets are retired.
- *After build 7.* **The tour.** A copy that has never been through it starts with it: eight cards over the page, sliding in one after the other with their rows settling in turn — what Glassine is (plain files, marks that step back, glass, nothing that tries too hard), then the backdrop behind the glass, a theme, the type, the caret's shape and glide, typewriter scrolling, focus and hidden syntax, and whether documents stay open as pages — each chosen on the card and seen at once on the page behind, through the same settings as Settings, so ⌘Z there takes any of it back. Return goes on, ← back, Esc leaves it; the demonstration copy and the check shots never see it. Help → **Tour of Glassine** and Settings → About → The tour bring it back. `tests/shot-syntax.sh tour N` photographs a card.
- *After build 7.* **Open pages.** A document opened stays open, as a page, until it is closed (⌘W, File → Close Page), and the pages sit in a stack with the one being written on top, kept between launches — with no row of tabs across the window. Hold ⌥ and press Tab: the page steps back and the open pages fan out across it as cards, each the page itself in little (its title and first lines in the theme's colours), in the order they were last used, the way ⌘Tab shows apps, with the page before this one already under the highlight. Tab steps along, ⇧Tab (or ←, →) back, ⌫ closes the highlighted page, Esc leaves things as they were, a click opens a card at once, and letting go of ⌥ opens the highlighted page — the other sliding aside as it comes in — so a tap of ⌥Tab flips to the page before. A row of pips beside the document's name in the footer shows the open pages, the one on screen lit; a click goes there, the pointer names them, and ⌥Tab with one page open gives them a small shake. The ⌘K command bar lists every open page by name, and *Previous page* and *Close page*. Trashing, shelving or renaming a page keeps the stack right. Settings → Library → **Keep pages open**, off, is one document at a time, as before. `tests/keys.sh` drives it by key press. See [pages](/library/pages).
- *After build 7.* **Tables in the editor.** A pipe table used to be lines of pipes and dashes, which is what it is in the file; the editor now shows it as a table. Every cell is measured as it is drawn and the narrower ones are padded — a kern on the cell's last space, or on its opening pipe for a right-aligned column — so each column is as wide as its widest cell, in any font; the pipes are drawn as hairlines the height of the row, the separator's dashes as a rule under the header, and the header in bold. The file is untouched. Tab goes to the next cell with its words selected, ⇧Tab to the one before, and Tab from the last cell adds a row; `/table` in the slash menu starts one. Review had tables already. See [tables](/editor/tables).
- *After build 7.* **A paste keeps its shape.** ⌘V read the clipboard's plain text, which is the one flavour on it that has lost its structure — Chrome, Word, Pages and Notes all drop the bullets and the numbers from it — so a pasted list arrived as bare lines. A paste now reads the rich flavour (RTF, or HTML) and writes what it finds as the Markdown the editor would have written: `-` and `1.` for the lists, nested by their depth and numbered from one; `#` for a heading set larger than the body; `**`, `*`, `~~` and `` ` `` for the weights; `[text](url)` for a link, bare when the text is the address; a pipe table for a table; a fence round a block of code. Plain text, when that is all there is, has its `•` turned to `-` and its `1)` to `1.`. Text copied out of Glassine carries a mark of its own and goes back in untouched. Edit → **Paste as Plain Text** (⌥⇧⌘V) is the raw string. `tests/pastetest.sh` runs the conversion over HTML and RTF as the clipboard carries them; `tests/pasteshot.sh` pastes into the editor itself.
- *After build 7.* **Move to Trash asks first.** ⌘⌫ sits a finger from ⌘⇧⌫ and the Delete key, and a tester lost the page he was writing to it (and found it in the Trash). Every way to the Trash — the key, the File menu, a row's or a card's menu — now puts up a sheet naming the document, or the folder and everything in it: Return moves it, Esc keeps it, and ⌘Z still puts it back afterwards.
- *After build 7.* **The page holds still while the sidebar comes and goes.** Showing or hiding the sidebar slides the editor to a new width over a few frames, and on each of them the text was wrapped twice — once at the new width less the old margins, a width nobody asked for, and again at the right one — while the scroll position stayed where it was. In a window narrow enough for the column to change, every line above the window rewrapped and the text in view jumped by as much, frame after frame; pressed quickly, the page sputtered. The column's width is now set in one place, once, only when it changes, and the line being looked at — the caret's, or the first one showing — is held where it is while the text reflows around it. The same holds for resizing the window.
- *After build 7.* **A new document is no longer filed as a daily note.** ⌘N puts a document in the folder last worked in, and opening a day's note made that folder Daily — so anyone who keeps daily notes found every new document landing among them. Daily is now never the folder a plain new document goes to: it goes to the top of the library instead. *New Document Here* on the Daily folder, and its **+**, still put one there.
- *After build 7.* **The month in the sidebar.** Under All Documents, Timelapse and New Document sits a calendar of the month, one cell a day, shaded by how much of that day's daily note there is — faint for a few lines, the full accent for a heavy day, bare where there is no note. The scale is the writer's own: a day is full at the 85th percentile of the days with a note (never under 150 words), so one long day does not flatten the rest. Today is ringed; the open note's day is ringed in the text colour; the note being written is counted as it is typed. Click a day to open its note or start one, ‹ › to turn the month, *today* to come back; the header folds it away. Word counts are taken at the library scan and cached with the tags and previews, so the calendar costs nothing to draw. See [the sidebar](/library/sidebar#the-calendar).
- *After build 7.* **Rows dragged into order.** A document in the sidebar's tree can be picked up and carried: it lifts under the pointer with a shadow, the rows it passes spring aside, and on release it glides into the gap. The order becomes **Your order**, a fourth sort beside last edited, name and date created — chosen by itself the moment a row is dragged, kept folder by folder, with unplaced documents at the top; ⌘Z takes a drag back. A row carried over a folder, or over the *Documents* header, moves there; the tree scrolls when a row is carried to its edge. Not system drag and drop: the row itself moves, no snapshot, no drop line, and the rows that are not being carried only redraw when the gap moves. See [dragging](/library/sidebar#dragging).
- *After build 7.* **Under the hood.** The library no longer polls. It watches its folder through the file system's own events and rescans a beat after anything changes — a file from another app, a sync landing, a save of its own — where before it walked the whole tree every three seconds whatever had happened, and a change from outside could take that long to show. A slow poll stays behind the events for what they can miss (a dataless iCloud file filling in, a volume that does not report), every thirty seconds and only while the app is active. A document is found by its id through an index rather than a search of the whole list; the sidebar's lists of open and starred documents, and the calendar's grid of days, are worked out once per scan or month instead of on every redraw; and a long document's word count is taken off the main thread when it opens or reloads, so a big file no longer holds the window while it is counted.
- *After build 7.* **⌘3 and ⌘D are today's note.** The third numbered row in the sidebar is now **Today** — the day's note, started if the day has none — with ⌘3 and ⌘D both going there; ⌥⌘D is retired (it was the system's Dock key). Timelapse keeps ⌘2 and gives up ⌘D; New Document keeps ⌘N and sits under the three, unnumbered. The Timelapse's *Start today's note* card, the command bar and the shortcut sheet say the new keys.
- *After build 7.* **The search bar no longer leaps as it goes.** Esc sent the ⌘F bar gliding back to its spot in the header, and then, for the last frames of its fade, it jumped to the middle of the window before vanishing. The overlay was taken down on a timer, a beat before the spring had landed, and the flags reset in the same update told the outgoing bar to come out again while it faded. Now the bar's place is its own, the app is told the moment the glide has landed and takes the overlay down then, with the header's field fading up under it; a ⌘F pressed while the bar is still on its way back starts a fresh one. The same shape was in the command bar: Return cleared the query as the card faded, so the list filled back in under it. The query is now reset when the bar next opens.
- *After build 7.* **A day's note starts under its heading.** A new daily note put its heading, an empty line, and then the caret on a third — so the day began with a blank paragraph nobody wrote. It now starts on the line right under the heading.
- *After build 7.* **Editorial's first paragraph is a paragraph.** The style set the paragraph after the headline larger, lighter and grey, as a magazine's standfirst; on ordinary writing it read as a heading that had wandered. It is now set like the rest.
- *After build 7.* **`---` is a rule the moment it is typed.** The third hyphen on a line of its own draws the rule and drops the caret to a fresh line under it; Return is no longer needed. Only the exact three at the end of a line — a longer run of dashes, or a rule being edited, is left alone.
- *After build 7.* **A rule stays a rule under the caret.** Clicking on a horizontal rule showed its three dashes until the caret left the line, the way the caret's line shows its marks. A rule has nothing in it to edit, so it now stays drawn with the caret on it; Backspace still takes it apart.
- *After build 7.* **Eight more Review styles.** Fourteen now: **Reader** (a quiet page, a serif, a narrow measure), **Gallery** (wall text, spaced capitals, white space), **Newspaper** (a broadsheet — headline across the page, two justified columns with a rule between, a drop cap; one column when narrow), **Typewriter** (Courier on cream paper, double spaced, emphasis underlined), **Notebook** (ruled paper, a red margin, blue-black ink, the writing on the lines), **Thesis** (Times, double spaced, sections numbered 1., 1.1), **Verse** (for poems — centred, every line a line, a blank line a stanza) and **Blueprint** (white line type on blueprint blue over a fine grid). The ones with their own paper — Book, Book Dark, Typewriter, Notebook, Blueprint — stay light or dark whatever the theme, and the pill over the page now knows that for all of them. For Verse, the page's HTML marks a block that follows a blank line (`data-gap`). See [Review](/review/review#styles).
- *After build 7.* **⌃Tab too.** Control-Tab brings up the open pages like Option-Tab does, ⌃⇧Tab steps back, and whichever modifier opened the carousel, letting that one go opens the highlighted page. The Settings caption, the shortcut sheet and the tour say both.
- *After build 7.* **A long page comes up at once.** Switching to a document of a few thousand words took several seconds. When a page came up the editor measured the line to hold still, which laid the text out; the layout manager grew the view to fit as it went, which came back into the same measurement, which laid out the rest of the document from inside the pass before it — a dozen nested passes on a long page. A guard against the re-entry: the page is immediate, and resizing the window on a long page is lighter for the same reason.
- *After build 7.* **The page beside the editor answers to ⌘⇧↩** rather than ⌥⌘↩ — one hand, for something reached for often.
- *After build 7.* **A quotation ends where its `>` lines end, and a list where its items end.** In Review — and on the page beside the editor, and in the PDF — the paragraphs after a quotation were drawn inside it, indented and in italics, and the paragraph after a list item, a task included, was drawn as part of that item, unless a blank line stood between. That was CommonMark's "lazy continuation", where a line after a quoted paragraph or a list item is taken as more of it; in Glassine every line is a paragraph of its own and the editor draws only the `>` lines as the quote and only the `-` lines as the list, so the page now does the same. A line indented under an item still belongs to it.
- *After build 7.* **The page beside the editor.** ⌘⇧↩ (View → Review Beside the Editor; the ⌘K command bar; the footer's menu) keeps Review to the right of the text instead of in place of it — the same rendering in the same style, and live: a beat after typing pauses the page takes the new text in place, without a reload, the scroll where it was, and it scrolls to the block the caret is in as you move through the document. Every top-level block the renderer writes now carries the source line it starts on (`data-line`), which is how the page knows where to go; the editor reports the caret's line a beat after it moves, only while a page is following. A hairline between the two is dragged to share the width; the share and the switch are kept between launches, like the sidebar's. ⌘⇧↩ again, or the × on the page's pill, puts it away; Esc stays the editor's; from Review, ⌘⇧↩ brings the editor back with the page beside it. See [Review](/review/review#beside-the-editor).
- *After build 7.* **The column of text is centred again after the sidebar comes or goes.** The text view is meant to be exactly as wide as the scroll view that holds it, and it followed the scroll view's width by autoresizing — which adds the *change* in the scroll view's width to whatever width the view began with. On macOS 27 the scroll view is made at no size at all and given its real size a moment later, so the text view ended up wider than the window's page by the 600 points it was born with, and the column was centred in that wider width: to the right of the middle, and by a different amount once the sidebar had been hidden or shown. The view now sets its width to the scroll view's outright whenever it lays its column out.
- *After build 7.* **A Welcome page for the app as it is.** The document a first launch creates when the library is empty had been written before backdrops, the formatting bar, chips, hidden syntax, Timelapse, the command bar, the remade Settings and sync existed, and named keys that had since moved. It now tours the app as it stands — the backdrop behind the page and where to change it, the bar over a selection and the slash menu, ⌃⌘M, ⌘1 and ⌘2, ⌘K and ⌘/, Review, ⌥⌘D and the date words, Settings and its search and ⌘Z, and the library on a second Mac through GitHub — in the same voice, ending the same way. Every key in it is the one the app answers to. And a first launch now opens on the page itself, from its heading, rather than on All Documents with one card in it; a document opened any other way still opens at its end, where the writing continues. A copy already made keeps the page it has; the demonstration copy's showcase carries the new one.
