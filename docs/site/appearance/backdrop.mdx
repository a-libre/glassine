The glass shows whatever is behind the window. With the right wallpaper that is the whole point of it: colour and light moving softly behind the page. With a plain desktop, or a picture that fights the text, it is not much of anything. A **backdrop** puts the colour inside the window instead — long folds of it, like silk lit from one side, each with a lit side, a shadowed side and a thin bright edge where it turns, drifting slowly — so the glass has something to look through wherever it is.

Settings → General → *Behind the glass*, or View → Backdrop, or *Backdrop: …* in the ⌘K command bar.

## The backdrops

| Backdrop | |
|---|---|
| **The desktop, through the glass** | The default: no backdrop, the window is a blur of what is behind it. |
| **Aurora** | The theme's own colours — its tint and its accent, run through the folds. Changes with the theme. |
| **Dusk** | Violet and indigo. |
| **Ocean** | Blue and teal. |
| **Ember** | Red, orange and gold. |
| **Moss** | Green and lime. |
| **Rose** | Pink, mauve and peach. |
| **Graphite** | Grey, barely tinted. |

Every backdrop takes its lightness from the theme, and is built dark first: on a dark theme the ground is close to black and the colour lives in the folds, deep rather than bright, so pale text stays readable over every part of it; a light theme gets the same folds as a pale morning. The theme's tint and grain still lie over it, and the sidebar frosts it the way it frosts the desktop.

The folds are drawn by a small program on the graphics processor — a field of noise warped through itself, read as bands and lit — at a quarter of the window's size, since there is nothing sharp in them, so they cost the writing nothing.

## Drift and frost

**Drift** moves the folds, slowly — the picture a minute from now is a different one, but no moment of it moves enough to catch the eye. Twenty frames a second while the window can be seen, none while it is covered. Off under Reduce Motion, and off with the switch: a still picture then.

**Frost** pales and softens the colour, the way frosted glass does: at 0 the folds are as deep as they get; at 100% they are a faint tint under a veil of light. 30% is the default.

Screenshots taken by the app of itself show the backdrop too, since it is inside the window; that is how the pictures on this page were made.
