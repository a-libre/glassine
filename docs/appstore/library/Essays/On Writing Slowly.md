# On Writing Slowly

There is a kind of attention that only arrives when nothing on the screen is asking for it. The cursor stops being a demand and becomes a place: the spot where the next word will go, whenever it comes.

Most tools for writing are really tools for *managing* writing. They count, they format, they suggest, they sync, and somewhere in all that helpfulness the sentence you were about to write quietly leaves the room. The alternative is not fewer features so much as fewer interruptions — a sheet of glass with words on it, and as little else as you can get away with.

## What slowness buys you

Slowness is not the same as getting less done. It is closer to *looking*. A slow writer reads the sentence back before moving on, hears its rhythm, notices the word that is doing no work. Speed hides these things. Speed is how you end up with three thousand words and nothing to say.

> The first draft is you telling yourself the story.

That line has been attributed to a dozen people, and every one of them was right. A draft is a conversation with yourself, and it goes better in a quiet room.

## A few habits that help

- Keep the line you are writing in the middle of the screen, where your eyes already are
- Let the rest of the page fade a little while you work
- Write the note for the day before the day starts asking for it
- Finish by reading the whole thing once, in a different typeface, the way a reader would

None of this needs an app. But an app can make the good habit the default and the bad one slightly harder, and over a year that adds up to a book.
